# Cover Letter

Dear Editor,

I am pleased to submit my manuscript, "Stolen Strata: Quantifying the Anthropogenic Erasure of Kashmir's Karewa Terraces and Its Threat to the Saffron Economy," for consideration as a Research Article in Applied Geography.

This study delineates 201 karewa terraces in Kashmir's Pampore-Pulwama-Budgam belt using a fully scripted, algorithmic pipeline (Topographic Position Index and slope thresholds on Copernicus DEM data), and tracks bare-earth land-cover change across four time slices from 1994 to 2025 using Landsat and Sentinel-2 imagery. It finds that bare-earth cover on the terraces is recent and accelerating (rising modestly until 2015, then more than tripling by 2025), statistically associated with proximity to roads and settlements, and located near saffron-growing terraces whose production value this study estimates using official state yield and price data. It also documents that, as of the most recent reporting located, no karewa-specific legislation in Jammu & Kashmir protects karewa land from excavation. I believe this combination of geomorphological quantification, statistical infrastructure-proximity testing, economic valuation, and policy context fits Applied Geography's scope in applying geospatial methods to a concrete, actionable land-use problem.

The manuscript is original, has not been published in a journal, and is not under consideration at any other journal. A preprint is posted on EarthArXiv (https://eartharxiv.org/repository/view/14805/). All data sources are cited, and the processing code and processed data are publicly available on GitHub (https://github.com/sakshimaske303-commits/STOLEN_STRATA) and archived on Zenodo (https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21766464).
